idea

The central thesis

One spec, three disasters

The hurricane spec and the fortress spec are the same spec.

Impact glazing rated for Category 5 debris is already multi-layer laminate; thicken the interlayer and you have rifle resistance with identical sightlines. Motorized storm shutters are motorized security shutters. ICF with an eight-inch reinforced core stops a hurricane-borne 2×4 at 100 mph and most of what a rifle does. The seismic detailing you need anyway produces progressive-collapse resistance for free.

Nothing has to look like a bunker, because the bunker is already load-bearing.

The conflict the design resolves

Hurricane wants you sealed — mass, shutters, no openings. Tsunami wants the opposite at ground level: a solid wall facing a wave is a wall that gets pushed over, so you let the water pass under the building.

The resolution is to split them by elevation. Seaward structures are open at grade and sealed above; everything hardened lives inland and high. Band B's ground level is carport, boat storage, outdoor kitchen and breezeway — designed to flood and be hosed out. Living space starts twelve to fifteen feet up on a concrete deck.

What the flood map actually says

That reasoning is sound but, on this parcel, largely unnecessary. FEMA's VE zone — the one with wave action, base flood elevation 3.4 m — stops eight metres inland of the waterline, and the tsunami evacuation zone stops at twelve. Both are seaward of the road. The buildable land begins at +7.2 m, in Zone X, outside both. The sacrificial ground floor is a good idea here, not a required one, and the document should say which.

Verification

What the survey data changed

This design was written from listing copy and satellite imagery. Before drawing any of it, the parcel was checked against primary sources: USGS elevation, the Planning Board's zoning and hazard layers, FEMA's flood mapping and NOAA's tidal datums. Six of the twelve open questions closed. Three of the answers change the design.

The lots are exactly what the listings said

The four solares are surveyed at 8,000.1571, 8,000.0006, 8,000.0005 and 8,061.2339 m² — 32,061.39 m², or 7.92 acres. The "approximately 2 acres each" figure and the "8,000 m² each" figure were never in conflict; 8,000 m² is 1.98 acres. There was no unit error to find.

The zoning is real, and it is not where the pin was

The district exists: DTS — Distrito Turístico Selectivo, on Suelo Rústico Común, catastro 429-000-006-01, barrio Montalva, under resolution JP-ZIT-71-72-01. The listings' shorthand "DT" is close enough.

But the coordinate this design was written around — 17.9488° N, 66.9130° W — is not in it. That point falls in B-Q, Bosque, classified SREP-E, Suelo Rústico Especialmente Protegido — Ecológico: the protected forest buffer of the Guánica biosphere reserve. The DTS land sits about 1.4 km west-southwest. Every drawing here uses the DTS parcel.

The waterfront is not yours to build on

This is the finding that actually binds. The design opens with Band A — the palapa, the fire pit, the boat ramp, the sea grape berm, "the paradise" — sitting on the water. On the real parcel that strip is zoned CR, Conservación de Recursos, and the road corridor runs between it and the buildable land. The DTS parcel does not reach the sea. It begins forty-four metres inland, at +7.2 m, behind a public road.

That does not kill the compound; it moves one band of it onto land the owner would have to negotiate for rather than buy. But it is the difference between a waterfront property and a property with a view, and no amount of design resolves it.

The Zona Marítimo-Terrestre, mapped

Puerto Rico inherited Spanish civil law here: the maritime-terrestrial zone is public domain, full stop, and its landward edge is set by the reach of the largest storm waves. MIPR does carry an official reference line — the Línea de Referencia Oficial — and it runs this coast. It is a reference, not a survey. Only a DRNA deslinde fixes the real boundary, and on a parcel whose seaward third is already conservation-zoned, that survey decides more here than the zoning table does.

The hazards are smaller than the design assumed

The 2020 M6.4 sequence was centred essentially at this location and the Muertos Trough lies directly offshore, so the tsunami case is real. But the Planning Board's evacuation zone reaches only +3.1 m, twelve metres inland, and FEMA's VE zone with its 3.4 m base flood elevation stops at eight. The headland is steep enough that the water has nowhere to run.

Everything hardened in this design already sits above +7 m and outside both lines. The vertical evacuation refuge on the roof terrace remains a sensible thing to own. It is no longer the thing the building is for.

The three bands survive, shifted inland

The gradient the whole scheme depends on is there. Across the 240 m of DTS land on this section the ground climbs from +7.2 m to +26.2 m — an eight per cent grade, steady, with no benching required. Paradise at the front, survival at the back, the house bridging them still works. It simply starts higher and further from the water than the brief imagined, and Band A is somebody else's land.
